SnapTune Privacy Notice

SnapTune is designed with privacy in mind. Most of the app works with data stored directly on your device. When you use an online feature, SnapTune may communicate with the service required for that feature.

This notice explains what SnapTune stores, what may leave your device, which Android permissions it uses, and how backups work.

Important: This notice describes the behavior of the SnapTune Android app based on its current source code and build configuration. It does not replace the privacy policies or terms of YouTube, YouTube Music, Discord, GitHub, lyrics providers, or any Together server you choose to use.

🔒 Privacy at a Glance

  • Most app data stays on your device. Your library, bookmarks, playlists, and settings live locally on your phone.
  • SnapTune does not secretly collect, sell, or broker your personal data.
  • SnapTune does not silently send your data to unrelated third-party services. There are no third-party telemetry trackers or analytics SDKs silently monitoring you.
  • Online features only send the information needed to provide that specific feature.
  • Data leaves your device when you choose to use a feature or integration that requires it.
  • Android backup and device-transfer features may copy some of SnapTune's local data depending on your OS configuration.
  • SnapTune also provides a manual backup export that you can start yourself.
  • The current Android manifest does not request access to your location, contacts, camera, calendar, SMS, or call logs.

📱 Data Stored on Your Device

SnapTune stores some information locally so features such as playback, your library, search, lyrics, synchronization, and customization can work properly.

Category Examples Why it is stored
Library & Playback Songs, artists, albums, playlists, likes, download status, play time, audio metadata Manage your local library, audio playback, offline downloads, and personal statistics.
Search & Lyrics Search queries, cached lyrics Search history and lyrics features.
Listening History Song IDs, timestamps, play time Listening history and user stats.
App Settings Language, country, UI, audio, proxy, cache, history settings, Together settings Personalization and feature configuration.
Optional YouTube Data Account name, email, channel handle, visitor data, sync ID, cookies, PO tokens Signed-in YouTube and YouTube Music features (only if you log in).
Optional Integrations Discord OAuth tokens and profile data, Together display name, client ID, session information Integrations you choose to explicitly enable.
Cached Files Streaming cache, download cache, other app files Faster playback, offline use, and smoother performance.

🌐 When Data Leaves Your Device

SnapTune does not continuously or silently forward your information to third-party services. External services are contacted only when you use a feature that needs them. The exact information sent depends on the feature and your configuration.

Service / Feature Data That May Be Sent When
YouTube / YouTube Music Search terms, playback requests, library/playlist requests, visitor data, sync IDs, cookies, or session/token values When browsing, streaming, syncing, or signing in.
Lyrics Providers Song title, artist, album identifiers, or similar lookup information When lyrics are requested for a playing track.
Discord Rich Presence Current song, artist, album, images, configured URLs/labels, and required Discord OAuth tokens When Rich Presence is enabled by you.
GitHub Releases Update-check requests and release metadata When checking for app updates or new releases.
Together Display name, client ID, session codes/keys, playback state, queue information, and room actions When hosting or joining a live Together listening session.

In short: If you don't use an online integration, SnapTune doesn't need to send that integration's data.

💾 Backups & Device Transfers

SnapTune currently supports Android's built-in backup system.

Some large or temporary files are excluded from backup and device transfer, including:

  • ExoPlayer cache
  • Download directory
  • exoplayer_internal.db

Other app data, such as local database content and app preferences, may still be included in Android backup or device transfer depending on your Android settings and device behavior.

Manual Backup

SnapTune also provides a manual backup/export feature.

When you start it, the app creates a ZIP archive containing app settings and database files. This export only happens when you explicitly trigger the backup action from settings.

🗑️ How Long Is Your Data Kept?

Local app data can remain on your device until you remove it.

You can remove local data at any time by:

  • Clearing SnapTune's app data in Android System Settings
  • Uninstalling SnapTune
  • Removing or overwriting data through available app features (e.g. clearing search history or deleting downloaded tracks)

Android's backup or device-transfer systems may also copy some app data to another device or restore it from a previous backup.

For Together and other third-party services, how long information is retained depends on the service or server you choose. Their own privacy and retention policies apply.

🎛️ Your Privacy Controls

You have control over many of SnapTune's privacy-related features. You can:

  • Use core features without enabling optional third-party integrations.
  • Choose whether to sign in to YouTube or YouTube Music.
  • Enable or disable Discord Rich Presence.
  • Enable or disable lyrics providers.
  • Choose whether to use Together.
  • Allow or deny Android permissions such as media, notification, and microphone access.
  • Configure or disable proxy settings.
  • Pause search history.
  • Pause listening history.
  • Create a local backup export.
  • Clear SnapTune's app data.
  • Uninstall SnapTune.

If you connected a third-party service, you may also need to revoke access or rotate tokens directly with that service.

🛡️ Security & Technical Notes

A few important technical details are worth mentioning:

Cleartext Traffic

The current Android manifest allows cleartext network traffic. This means that a feature or configured endpoint could use HTTP instead of HTTPS where supported.

Audio Playback Capture

The current Android manifest also enables Android's audio playback capture capability. Under Android's platform rules, compatible system features or authorized applications may be able to capture SnapTune's audio playback.

Local Encryption

The current codebase does not clearly document encryption at rest for the app's:

  • Database
  • Preferences
  • Cache files

Therefore, SnapTune does not make a promise that locally stored data is encrypted at rest.

Third-Party Services

This notice describes the behavior of the SnapTune Android app. Third-party services — including YouTube, YouTube Music, Discord, GitHub, lyrics providers, and Together servers — may have their own logging, data retention, security practices, and privacy policies.

If you use a self-hosted or third-party Together server, its operator may have additional data practices.

Future Changes

If future versions of SnapTune add new integrations, SDKs, permissions, network services, data flows, or backup behavior, this privacy notice should be updated accordingly.

🔄 Changes to This Privacy Notice

This notice should be reviewed whenever SnapTune changes its permissions, local storage or database structure, external integrations, backup behavior, or network architecture.

The "Last updated" date at the top will be changed when the notice is updated.
